TITLE: RBI Warning on Fictitious Offers of Lottery Winnings and Cheap Funds
SOURCE: Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
CONTENT: The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has repeatedly warned the public against falling prey to fictitious offers of huge lottery winnings, cheap funds, or international prize money. Fraudsters often send letters, emails, or call victims claiming they have won a lottery (such as KBC lottery draws) or that an unclaimed inheritance is waiting for them. The victim is then asked to deposit an "upfront processing fee", "transfer tax", or "clearance charge" into a specific bank account. The RBI states that it never stores money for individuals, does not run any lucky draws, and never requests processing fees to release winnings. Any demand for prepayment to receive funds is a scam.

TITLE: RBI Directive on KYC Update Phishing and Account Suspension Threats
SOURCE: Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
CONTENT: The RBI has issued guidelines to caution bank customers against fake calls, messages, or emails threatening immediate suspension or blocking of their bank accounts, debit cards, or digital wallets. In these scams, callers pose as bank managers, executives, or RBI officers and tell the victim that their KYC (Know Your Customer) has expired. They create panic and urgency, demanding that the victim perform KYC updates over the phone or click a link. The RBI reiterates that commercial banks and financial institutions never request sensitive credentials—including One-Time Passwords (OTPs), PINs, CVV codes, or passwords—via phone calls or text messages. Any call demanding an OTP to verify identity or stop account blocking is fraudulent.

TITLE: RBI Caution on Unauthorized Digital Lending and Loan Apps
SOURCE: Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
CONTENT: The RBI warns members of the public against signing up for unauthorized digital lending apps that promise instant, paperless loans without credit checks. These platforms often charge usurious interest rates, hidden processing fees, and employ illegal, aggressive recovery tactics. Some callers masquerade as recovery agents or bank representatives, requesting bank transfers, processing fees, or threat-based repayments. The RBI advises customers to verify that any lending service is registered as a bank or Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) before sharing personal documents or making transfers.
