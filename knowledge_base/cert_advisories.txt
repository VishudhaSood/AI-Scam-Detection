TITLE: CERT-In Advisory on Customs Parcel and Law Enforcement Impersonation Scams
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)
CONTENT: CERT-In has issued a high-priority warning regarding online scams involving impersonation of law enforcement agencies, customs officials, or courier services (like FedEx or DHL). In these scams, victims receive telephone calls claiming that a package sent in their name was intercepted by customs or police. They are told the parcel contains illegal items like drugs, contraband, or fake passports. To create extreme fear, the fraudsters pose as police officers, CBI agents, or narcotics bureau staff via video call, showing fake arrest warrants and threatening immediate jail. The scammers then demand a "settlement fee" or "security deposit" to clear the victim's name, promising to refund it after verification. CERT-In clarifies that government and police departments never demand money, bank transfers, or settlements over phone/video calls to clear legal charges.

TITLE: CERT-In Caution on Remote Access Software and Tech Support Fraud
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)
CONTENT: CERT-In cautions Internet users against fake tech support scams where callers claim to be from Microsoft, Apple, or antivirus companies. The scammers warn that the victim's computer is infected with malware or that their bank account is being hacked. They instruct the victim to download remote access applications such as AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or UltraViewer to let the "expert" fix the problem. Once access is granted, the fraudsters steal bank logins, passwords, and sensitive personal information, or lock the victim's screen and demand a ransom. Users are warned never to install remote access tools on instructions from unsolicited callers.

TITLE: CERT-In Advisory on Aadhaar-Enabled Payment System (AePS) Phishing
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)
CONTENT: CERT-In warns citizens against AePS fraud where attackers clone fingerprints and access biometric details of individuals. Fraudsters utilize public government land registry websites or silicone molds to clone fingerprint impressions. Using these cloned fingerprints and Aadhaar card numbers, they authorize illegal cash withdrawals from the victim's bank account via AePS micro-ATMs. Citizens are advised to lock their biometrics using the mAadhaar app or UIDAI website to prevent unauthorized withdrawals.
