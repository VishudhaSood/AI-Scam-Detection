TITLE: CERT-In Advisory on Customs Parcel and Law Enforcement Impersonation Scams
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)
CONTENT: CERT-In has issued a high-priority warning regarding online scams involving impersonation of law enforcement agencies, customs officials, or courier services (like FedEx or DHL). In these scams, victims receive telephone calls claiming that a package sent in their name was intercepted by customs or police. They are told the parcel contains illegal items like drugs, contraband, or fake passports. To create extreme fear, the fraudsters pose as police officers, CBI agents, or narcotics bureau staff via video call, showing fake arrest warrants and threatening immediate jail. The scammers then demand a "settlement fee" or "security deposit" to clear the victim's name, promising to refund it after verification. CERT-In clarifies that government and police departments never demand money, bank transfers, or settlements over phone/video calls to clear legal charges.

TITLE: CERT-In Caution on Remote Access Software and Tech Support Fraud
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)
CONTENT: CERT-In cautions Internet users against fake tech support scams where callers claim to be from Microsoft, Apple, or antivirus companies. The scammers warn that the victim's computer is infected with malware or that their bank account is being hacked. They instruct the victim to download remote access applications such as AnyDesk, TeamViewer, or UltraViewer to let the "expert" fix the problem. Once access is granted, the fraudsters steal bank logins, passwords, and sensitive personal information, or lock the victim's screen and demand a ransom. Users are warned never to install remote access tools on instructions from unsolicited callers.

TITLE: CERT-In Advisory on Aadhaar-Enabled Payment System (AePS) Phishing
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)
CONTENT: CERT-In warns citizens against AePS fraud where attackers clone fingerprints and access biometric details of individuals. Fraudsters utilize public government land registry websites or silicone molds to clone fingerprint impressions. Using these cloned fingerprints and Aadhaar card numbers, they authorize illegal cash withdrawals from the victim's bank account via AePS micro-ATMs. Citizens are advised to lock their biometrics using the mAadhaar app or UIDAI website to prevent unauthorized withdrawals.

TITLE: CERT-In / MHA Advisory on Digital Arrest Scams
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) / Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA)
CONTENT: CERT-In and the Ministry of Home Affairs have issued a joint advisory on the growing phenomenon of "Digital Arrest" scams in India. In a digital arrest scam, fraudsters impersonate officers of the CBI, Narcotics Bureau, Customs, Cyber Crime Police, or Income Tax Department. They call or video-call the victim claiming that a parcel in their name has been intercepted containing drugs, that their Aadhaar card has been used in money laundering, or that a case is registered against them. The victim is told they are under "digital arrest" — instructed to remain on the video call continuously and not speak to anyone. Using fake court orders, arrest warrants, and official-looking backgrounds, the scammers intimidate the victim into transferring large sums of money as "settlement fees," "security deposits," or "bail amounts." PM Modi has specifically warned citizens: "No government agency contacts you via phone or video call for such matters. There is no system called digital arrest in our law." If you receive such a call, disconnect immediately, do not transfer any money, and report the incident on the Cyber Crime helpline 1930 or the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in).

TITLE: CERT-In Advisory on Matrimonial Portal & NRI Romance Financial Extortion Scams
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)
CONTENT: CERT-In cautions citizens against matrimony and romance scams operating on matrimonial portals (Shaadi.com, BharatMatrimony) and social media. Fraudsters create fake profiles claiming to be wealthy NRIs, doctors, or defense officers living abroad. After gaining emotional trust, they claim to send expensive gifts, jewelry, or foreign currency that is purportedly held up at New Delhi airport customs. A fake customs officer then calls demanding "customs clearance duties" or "foreign currency exchange taxes". Citizens are warned never to send money to individuals met online.

TITLE: CERT-In Warning on Fake Travel Booking, Hotel Reservations & Airline Ticket Phishing
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In)
CONTENT: CERT-In warns travelers against cloned booking websites and fraudulent WhatsApp customer support accounts pretending to be IRCTC, MakeMyTrip, Indigo, or luxury resort chains. Scammers advertise heavily discounted holiday packages and demand full advance payment via UPI or bank transfer. When travelers arrive at the hotel or airport, they find no booking exists. Users are advised to verify domain URLs carefully and only book through official verified mobile apps.

TITLE: CERT-In Caution on Government Housing Allotment & Real Estate Advance Token Scams
SOURCE: Indian Computer Emergency Response Team (CERT-In) / RERA
CONTENT: CERT-In and RERA issue a security advisory on fake government housing schemes (DDA, MHADA, PM Awas Yojana) and bogus plot allotment schemes. Fraudsters send fake allotment letters on official letterheads and demand immediate token deposits or processing fees. Citizens are urged to verify housing scheme allotments through official state portal dashboards before transferring funds.
